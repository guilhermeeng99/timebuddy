Under the clock face

Time is harder than it looks

Every rule below exists because the naive version quietly produces a wrong hour for somebody, some month a year. They are written down, tested against real historical transitions, and enforced in one place.

Not every offset is a whole hour

India is +05:30, Nepal +05:45, the Chatham Islands +12:45. Those rows print their minutes instead of being rounded into a column they do not belong to.

A day is not always 24 hours

On the days the clocks move it is 23 or 25. The grid asks the time zone engine how many hours the day actually has instead of counting to 24, so those days line up like any other.

Some times never happen. Some happen twice

On a spring-forward date an hour is skipped; on a fall-back date one is repeated. The converter says so above the answer, with a toggle for the second occurrence — rather than silently answering a different question.

A zone that disappears is flagged, never guessed

Time zone identifiers get renamed and retired. When a saved city's zone stops resolving, its row is kept and marked — because a clock that reads plausibly and belongs to somewhere else is the worst possible answer.

Open source

Flutter, clean architecture, one specification per feature. Read the code, run it yourself, or fork it — the conventions are written down, not implied.

The same board everywhere

Two small documents per account, reconciled by revision number. Local is always the read path, so the app opens instantly and works offline.

Tested where it matters

Every time zone rule is pinned by a test against a real historical or scheduled transition, never a synthetic one. The clock is injected, so no test reads the wall.
